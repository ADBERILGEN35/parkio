# Problem

## The Gap

Navigation apps can route a driver to a destination. They usually cannot tell the driver whether a nearby real-world parking spot is available, legal, recent, photo-backed, and suitable for their vehicle. That last-mile uncertainty is the problem Parkio targets.

## What Drivers Need To Know

- Is there a usable spot near my destination right now?
- Is it still available, or has someone already taken it?
- Is it legal, restricted, or risky?
- Will my vehicle fit?
- Does a recent photo support the claim?
- Has another driver verified, claimed, or reported it?

## Why This Hurts

Missing or stale parking information makes drivers circle blocks, repeat searches, make rushed decisions, and rely on fragmented local knowledge. Drivers often see useful parking information, but unless it is captured immediately it disappears before the next person can use it.

## Why Existing Tools Do Not Fully Solve It

Google Maps, Apple Maps, navigation apps, municipal apps, paid parking platforms, and community groups each solve part of the experience. They are not usually centered on community-confirmed, photo-backed, street-level availability with freshness, vehicle fit, trust, claiming, reporting, and moderation.

This is a category gap, not a claim that competitors cannot add similar capabilities.

## User Stories

- As a commuter, I want to see recently shared parking spots near my destination so I can reduce circling.
- As a driver leaving a spot, I want to share it quickly so someone nearby can use it.
- As a cautious driver, I want legal status, vehicle fit, and photo context before trusting a spot.
- As a community member, I want to verify or report a spot so bad data does not stay visible.
- As a returning driver, I want opt-in Smart Return help when I expect to need parking later.
- As a beta tester, I want clear known limitations and a structured way to report issues.

## What Must Be Validated

- Whether drivers will contribute spots at the right moment.
- Whether shared spots remain useful long enough for another driver.
- Whether verification and claiming can improve trust without creating abuse.
- Whether one geography can reach enough density to make the product useful.
