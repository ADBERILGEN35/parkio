# Landing Page User Flow

This document defines how different visitors should move through the `parkio.dev` landing page.

## Universal Flow

1. Visitor arrives at `parkio.dev`.
2. Visitor understands Parkio from the hero.
3. Visitor scans the problem and solution.
4. Visitor checks trust, privacy, and beta status.
5. Visitor chooses a CTA.
6. Visitor either joins the waitlist, reads documentation, follows development, or contacts the founder.

## Primary Visitor: Driver or Beta Tester

Goal: decide whether Parkio is relevant and whether to join the waitlist.

Expected path:

1. Hero
2. How It Works
3. Features
4. Trust and Privacy
5. Hosted Beta
6. FAQ
7. Join beta waitlist

Potential concerns:

- Is it live?
- Does it track me?
- Is my city supported?
- What happens after I join?
- Is this safe to use while driving?

Design response:

- Keep availability status explicit.
- Explain privacy principles before the form.
- Avoid implying immediate access.
- Make beta expectations clear.

## Startup Program Reviewer

Goal: understand the startup, technical credibility, and cloud-credit use case.

Expected path:

1. Hero
2. Problem
3. Technology Credibility
4. Hosted Beta
5. View technical documentation

Potential concerns:

- Is the product real?
- What stage is it in?
- Are credits tied to concrete milestones?
- Is the team honest about limitations?

Design response:

- Link to `docs/startup/` and certification evidence.
- Keep claims evidence-based.
- Present hosted-beta readiness without public-production claims.

## Investor or Angel Visitor

Goal: decide whether Parkio is worth a conversation.

Expected path:

1. Hero
2. Problem
3. Solution
4. Hosted Beta
5. Contact founder

Potential concerns:

- Is the problem clear?
- Who is the first user?
- What has been built?
- What is still unvalidated?

Design response:

- Be concise and direct.
- Do not bury the stage.
- State that traction, revenue, and market metrics are not yet measured unless later evidence exists.

## Technical Due Diligence Visitor

Goal: verify technical seriousness and readiness boundaries.

Expected path:

1. Hero
2. Technology Credibility
3. Trust and Privacy
4. Documentation

Potential concerns:

- Is the architecture coherent?
- What has been certified?
- What remains blocked for public production?
- What privacy and security posture exists?

Design response:

- Provide direct documentation links.
- Keep technical claims specific.
- Avoid dense diagrams on the public page; let documentation carry detail.

## Exit Paths

Acceptable exits:

- Waitlist submitted.
- Documentation opened.
- Contact initiated.
- Development followed.
- Visitor leaves with clear understanding that public production is not live yet.

Unacceptable exits:

- Visitor believes Parkio is already publicly launched.
- Visitor expects immediate parking availability in their city.
- Visitor assumes location tracking behavior that is not documented.
- Reviewer sees unsupported traction or revenue claims.
